How to shoot a set that's actually diagnostic
  • Trot, in hand, on a loose lead. A tight lead masks the head nod, which is the single most useful sign in the whole set.
  • Hard, flat, level ground. A driveway or aisle beats an arena for spotting foot pain.
  • Camera still on a fence post or tripod. Don't pan, don't zoom, don't walk with the horse — let it move through a fixed background so vertical movement can be measured against something stationary.
  • Front and rear: trot 25–30 m straight toward the camera, then straight away. Keep the whole horse in frame.
  • Side: square to the track, poll, withers and croup all visible and unobstructed, 6–8 consecutive strides in one take.
  • 8–15 seconds each, good light, no long shadow stripes across the ground.
