🐎 Stride

Upload a video of your horse in motion. Get a ranked list of what might be going on, and what it takes to get them striding out again.

This is a screening aid, not a diagnosis. It reads still frames from your video and cannot palpate, feel a digital pulse, use hoof testers, flex a joint or run a nerve block — the things that actually confirm a lameness. Use it to know what to look at and what to ask. Anything painful, sudden or worsening needs a veterinarian.

How to shoot a clip that's actually diagnostic
  • Trot, in hand, on a loose lead. A tight lead masks the head nod, which is the single most useful sign in the whole clip.
  • Hard, flat, level ground. A driveway or aisle beats an arena for spotting foot pain.
  • Film from the side first for stride length and head carriage — then straight toward and straight away from the camera.
  • Get the whole horse in frame for at least 8–10 seconds, and hold the camera still. Let the horse move past you rather than panning.
  • Good light, no long shadows. Film the same clip on a circle in both directions if you can — many lamenesses only show on a turn.
