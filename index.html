The overlooked risks

12 hazards hiding in an ordinary home

None of these look dangerous on a normal day. That's exactly why they're missed. Numbers match the floor plan above.

1
Bathroom

The tub-and-shower combo

Stepping over a 15-inch tub wall on wet, glazed tile is the single most common setup for a serious fall at home.

2
Bathroom

Towel bars and low toilets

Towel bars and soap dishes get grabbed in a slip but aren't anchored to hold body weight. Standard 15-inch toilets are hard on knees and hips.

3
Living areas

Slick tile, terrazzo and sunken rooms

Polished floors turn slippery with humidity or a spilled drink. Sunken living rooms add a single, easy-to-miss step.

Common in South Florida
4
Front entry

Raised entry steps

Homes here sit on raised slabs for flood elevation, so nearly every entrance has a step or two, often without a railing.

Common in South Florida
5
Lanai

Sliding-door tracks and thresholds

The raised track on a sliding glass door is a classic trip point for shuffling feet, walkers and wheelchairs alike.

Common in South Florida
6
Garage

The step down to the garage

Laundry often lives in the garage here, so it's a trip you make while carrying a full basket that blocks your view of the step.

Common in South Florida
7
Hallways & doors

Narrow doorways, round knobs

Older 28–30 inch doorways won't fit a walker or wheelchair comfortably. Round knobs are hard to turn with arthritis.

8
Bedroom to bath

The dark path at night

Most nighttime falls happen on the walk from bed to bathroom: groggy, in the dark, with switches on the wrong side of the room.

9
Kitchen

Reaching high and bending low

Upper cabinets lead to step stools. Deep lower cabinets mean kneeling. Back-mounted stove knobs mean reaching over burners.

10
Whole home

Hurricane season with limited mobility

Heavy storm panels, manual garage doors, power-dependent medical equipment and evacuation routes all get harder with age.

June 1 – Nov 30
11
Pool & deck

Wet decks and pool access

Slick deck coatings, steep pool steps and no barrier for visiting grandkids. A pool is a joy and a real hazard at the same time.

Common in South Florida
12
Whole home

No way to call for help

A fall when you live alone can mean hours on the floor. In our heat, a failed AC unit that nobody notices is a health emergency too.

Peace of mind

Freedom for you. Relief for the people who love you.

For you

  • Stay in your own homeYour neighbors, your church, your doctors, your routines. No forced move to assisted living.
  • Keep your independenceShower, cook and get around on your own terms, without waiting on someone to help.
  • Move with confidenceFear of falling makes people move less, which makes falls more likely. A safer home breaks that cycle.
  • Protect your savingsModifying your home typically costs far less than a single year of assisted living or a long rehab stay after a fall.
  • A home that still feels like homeGrab bars that look like designer hardware and showers you'd choose anyway. Nothing that says "hospital."

For your family

  • Fewer worried phone callsEspecially for children and grandchildren who live out of state and can't drop by.
  • Know help is one button awayAlerts reach family if there's a fall, a door left open, or the AC fails in August.
  • Ready before a crisisPlanning now beats scrambling after a hospital discharge, when decisions get rushed.
  • Easier caregivingWider doors and roll-in showers make helping a loved one safer for the caregiver, too.
  • Visits everyone enjoysA home that works for grandparents, toddlers and everyone in between.

Our solutions

Every risk has a fix, and most are simpler than you think

From a one-day grab bar install to a full bathroom conversion, every project starts with your home and your needs. Nothing is one-size-fits-all.

AreaWhat we seeWhat we do

Bathroom safety

What we see
  • High tub walls (1)
  • Unrated towel bars, low toilets (2)
What we do
  • Curbless, walk-in or roll-in showers
  • Tub cut-outs and walk-in tubs
  • Blocked and anchored grab bars, placed to fit you
  • Comfort-height toilets, bidet seats
  • Fold-down shower seats and handheld showers
  • Anti-scald valves, slip-resistant tile

Floors & transitions

What we see
  • Slick tile and terrazzo, sunken rooms (3)
  • Sliding-door tracks (5)
What we do
  • Slip-resistant treatments for existing tile and terrazzo
  • New slip-rated, low-glare flooring
  • Leveling sunken rooms or adding rails and contrast edging
  • Flush thresholds and low-profile door tracks

Entries & steps

What we see
  • Raised entry steps (4)
  • Garage step-down (6)
What we do
  • Zero-step entries and gently sloped walkways
  • Code-compliant ramps that blend with the house
  • Handrails on both sides of every step
  • Laundry relocation inside the home

Doors & mobility

What we see
  • Narrow doorways, round knobs (7)
What we do
  • Widened 36-inch doorways and pocket doors
  • Lever handles and easy-turn hardware
  • Offset hinges, keyless and remote entry
  • Stair lifts and home elevators for two-story homes

Lighting

What we see
  • Dark path from bed to bath (8)
What we do
  • Motion-activated night path lighting
  • Lighted rocker switches at every doorway
  • Brighter, glare-free fixtures and under-cabinet lights

Kitchen

What we see
  • High shelves, deep cabinets, back-mounted knobs (9)
What we do
  • Pull-down upper shelving and pull-out drawers
  • Varied counter heights and seated work areas
  • Front-control cooktops and wall ovens
  • Lever and touchless faucets

Hurricane readiness

What we see
  • Heavy shutters, power outages, evacuation (10)
What we do
  • Impact windows and doors, so nothing to put up
  • Motorized or accordion shutters you can close alone
  • Standby generators for medical equipment and AC
  • Battery-backup garage door openers
  • A clear, step-free exit route

Pool & outdoor

What we see
  • Wet decks, steep pool steps (11)
What we do
  • Slip-resistant deck resurfacing
  • Pool handrails, wide entry steps and lifts
  • Self-latching pool barriers and door alarms
  • Well-lit, level paths around the house

Smart home & alerts

What we see
  • No way to call for help (12)
What we do
  • Fall detection and medical alert systems
  • Video doorbells and smart locks family can manage
  • Smart thermostats that alert family if the AC fails
  • Water-leak and stove shut-off sensors
